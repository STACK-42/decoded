# Affine Ciphers

A shift cipher only adds. If we multiply as well, we get a larger key space, and the lesson on modular inverses suddenly matters: multiplying only works as a cipher when you can undo it.

## The cipher

An **affine transformation** uses a pair of keys, $k_1$ and $k_2$:

$$
E_{(k_1, k_2)}(m) = k_1 m + k_2 \pmod{26}
$$

To decrypt, subtract $k_2$ and then multiply by the inverse of $k_1$:

$$
D_{(k_1, k_2)}(c) = k_1^{-1}(c - k_2) \pmod{26}
$$

The inverse only exists when $\gcd(k_1, 26) = 1$, and this is a hard requirement. Drag $k_1$ and watch the lines. When $k_1$ shares a factor with 26, they bunch up on fewer letters, and that information is gone for good:

## Worked example: encrypting

Encrypt SECURITY with $k_1 = 7$ and $k_2 = 21$:

| Letter | $m$ | $7m + 21$ | mod 26 | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S | 18 | 147 | 17 | R |
| E | 4 | 49 | 23 | X |
| C | 2 | 35 | 9 | J |
| U | 20 | 161 | 5 | F |
| R | 17 | 140 | 10 | K |
| I | 8 | 77 | 25 | Z |
| T | 19 | 154 | 24 | Y |
| Y | 24 | 189 | 7 | H |

So SECURITY becomes RXJFKZYH.

## Worked example: decrypting

To decrypt VLXIJH, first find the inverse of 7 modulo 26. It's 15, since $7 \cdot 15 = 105 = 4 \cdot 26 + 1$:

| Letter | $c$ | $15(c - 21)$ | mod 26 | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| V | 21 | 0 | 0 | A |
| L | 11 | -150 | 6 | G |
| X | 23 | 30 | 4 | E |
| I | 8 | -195 | 13 | N |
| J | 9 | -180 | 2 | C |
| H | 7 | -210 | 24 | Y |

The message is AGENCY.

## How strong is it?

There are 12 valid values for $k_1$, because $\phi(26) = 12$, and 26 for $k_2$. That makes $12 \times 26 = 312$ keys, which a computer tries instantly. It also maps each letter to one fixed letter, so letter frequencies still give it away.

<Callout tone="sky" label="Try it yourself">
Use $k_1 = 11$ and $k_2 = 23$ to encrypt THE NATIONAL SECURITY AGENCY. The inverse of 11 modulo 26 is 19, so you can check your answer by decrypting with $19(c - 23)$.
</Callout>
